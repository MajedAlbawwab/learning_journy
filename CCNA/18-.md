# Copper Cables

<span style="color:#3b82f6">layer 1 technologies</span> (based on <span style="color:#3b82f6">TCP/IP model</span>)
<span style="color:#e07b39">physical links/connections</span>:

## A- copper (ethernet)

the oldest, variety in speed, developed through time

* 4 pairs of copper
* functioning like an electric circuit
* 2 pairs for 200 Mbps
* 4 pairs for 1000 Mbps
* shielded (<span style="color:#3b82f6">STP</span>) and unshielded (<span style="color:#3b82f6">UTP</span>)
* connector <span style="color:#3b82f6">RJ45</span>

## B- optical fibers

new, already in high speeds

* single fiber is enough
* starts with 1 Gbps, up to tens of Gbps
* either <span style="color:#3b82f6">light</span> or <span style="color:#3b82f6">laser</span>

2 types of transmission media are used: light or laser
<span style="color:#3b82f6">multimode (MM)</span>: light for short distance
<span style="color:#3b82f6">single mode (SM)</span>: laser for long distance

connectors: on the end of each fiber optic cable
<span style="color:#22c55e">LC, SC, FC, MTP/MPO</span>

<span style="color:#e07b39">SFP (Small Form-Factor Pluggable)</span>
Definition: An <span style="color:#e07b39">SFP</span> is a <span style="color:#e07b39">removable transceiver module</span> used to connect a switch or router to a network through fiber-optic or copper cabling.
The module converts signals between the device's electrical interface and the transmission medium—for example, <span style="color:#22c55e">electrical signals to optical signals for fiber</span>
