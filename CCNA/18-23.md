# Copper Cables

<span style="color:#3b82f6">layer 1 technologies</span> (based on <span style="color:#3b82f6">TCP/IP model</span>)
<span style="color:#e07b39">physical links/connections</span>:

## A- copper (ethernet)

the oldest, variety in speed, developed through time

* 4 pairs of copper
* functioning like an electric circuit
* 2 pairs for 200 Mbps
* 4 pairs for 1000 Mbps
* shielded (<span style="color:#3b82f6">STP</span>) and unshielded (<span style="color:#3b82f6">UTP</span>)
* connector <span style="color:#3b82f6">RJ45</span>

## B- optical fibers

new, already in high speeds

* single fiber is enough
* starts with 1 Gbps, up to tens of Gbps
* either <span style="color:#3b82f6">light</span> or <span style="color:#3b82f6">laser</span>

2 types of transmission media are used: light or laser
<span style="color:#3b82f6">multimode (MM)</span>: light for short distance
<span style="color:#3b82f6">single mode (SM)</span>: laser for long distance

connectors: on the end of each fiber optic cable
<span style="color:#22c55e">LC, SC, FC, MTP/MPO</span>

<span style="color:#e07b39">SFP (Small Form-Factor Pluggable)</span>
Definition: An <span style="color:#e07b39">SFP</span> is a <span style="color:#e07b39">removable transceiver module</span> used to connect a switch or router to a network through fiber-optic or copper cabling.
The module converts signals between the device's electrical interface and the transmission medium—for example, <span style="color:#22c55e">electrical signals to optical signals for fiber</span>
  
 -------------------------------------------------------------------
<span style="color:#e07b39">broadcasting</span> is a communication method where a single device sends a message to all devices on the network simultaneously
a broadcast can not go passed routers

## point to point & shared media

* <span style="color:#3b82f6">point to point (P2P)</span>: directly connected, nothing in the way
* <span style="color:#3b82f6">shared media</span>, <span style="color:#e07b39">broadcast</span>, a layer 2 device in the way

## Power Over Ethernet (PoE)

Definition: <span style="color:#e07b39">PoE</span> allows Ethernet cables to carry both electrical power and network data to a connected device.

* carrying power over 2 pairs of copper cables (enough to power up some devices)
* can replace an AC adapter
* PoE terms:
  * <span style="color:#3b82f6">PSE</span>: power sourcing equipment (<span style="color:#22c55e">switches, power injectors</span>)
  * <span style="color:#3b82f6">PD</span>: powered device (<span style="color:#22c55e">PC, IP camera ...</span>)
## 1. PoE Classification Process

### Step 1 – <span style="color:#e07b39">Power Detection</span>

Do not supply power to a <span style="color:#3b82f6">PoE</span>-capable port until <span style="color:#e07b39">power detection</span> identifies that the connected device needs power.

### Step 2 – <span style="color:#e07b39">Layer 1 Power Detection</span>

The <span style="color:#3b82f6">PSE</span> detects the presence of a <span style="color:#3b82f6">PD</span>.

It sends low-power signals and monitors the returned signals to determine whether the device is a <span style="color:#3b82f6">PD</span>.

### Step 3 – <span style="color:#e07b39">Layer 1 Power Classification</span>

If a <span style="color:#3b82f6">PD</span> is identified, the <span style="color:#3b82f6">PSE</span> determines an initial <span style="color:#e07b39">power level</span> based on <span style="color:#e07b39">Layer 1 power classification</span>.

It supplies that <span style="color:#e07b39">power level</span> so the <span style="color:#3b82f6">PD</span> can perform basic software initialization.

### Step 4 – <span style="color:#e07b39">Layer 2 Power Classification</span>

Uses <span style="color:#3b82f6">LLDP</span> or <span style="color:#3b82f6">CDP</span>.

The <span style="color:#3b82f6">PD</span> announces a specific <span style="color:#e07b39">power class</span>.

If a new <span style="color:#e07b39">power class</span> is selected, the <span style="color:#3b82f6">PSE</span> adjusts the <span style="color:#e07b39">power level</span>.

### PoE Power Management Modes

* <span style="color:#3b82f6">Auto</span>: The switch automatically detects a powered device (PD) and supplies the required power if available.
* <span style="color:#3b82f6">Static</span>: The switch reserves a configured amount of power for the port, even before the device uses it.
* <span style="color:#3b82f6">Never</span>: The switch disables PoE on that port.

## Collisions

* More than one device (<span style="color:#22c55e">PC</span>) transmitting at a single time in a <span style="color:#3b82f6">shared media</span>.
* <span style="color:#ef4444">Causes collisions and data loss.</span>
* To avoid collisions:
  * <span style="color:#3b82f6">CSMA/CD</span> (Carrier Sense Multiple Access/Collision Detection)
    * Listen for transmission and wait for your turn.
    * Used with <span style="color:#3b82f6">half-duplex</span>.
  * <span style="color:#3b82f6">Full-duplex</span>
    * Bidirectional transmission: sending and receiving simultaneously.
* Errors
  * <span style="color:#ef4444">Cabling issues, unsupported SFP.</span>
* <span style="color:#ef4444">Duplex mismatch</span>
  * Half or full? Must match.
* Speed
  * 10 / 100 / 1000 Mbps — must be compatible at both ends.
